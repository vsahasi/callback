Where these numbers come from

I generated this page by driving the detector over its own HTTP interface in real mode, four callers against two states of the voice record, and writing down what came back. The sentences under each signal are the detector's, not mine. If a threshold moves, the page moves with it.

Why a clone is only challenged

A clone of the vendor is supposed to match on voice. That signal answers its own question correctly and carries the heaviest weight, so the two signals that did catch it end up a smaller share of the total. What stops a clone is the code texted to the number on the invoice, not the blended score.

Why a recording is denied at 0.17

That is below the line where a caller passes, and it is denied anyway. Three rules hold at once: the replay score sits just above the threshold that would make it fatal on its own, a failing signal can never become an allow, and replay is one of two signals a correct code cannot talk its way past. Take away any one and a recording gets through.

What it cannot do

This is not speaker verification. It compares pitch register and long-term spectral shape, which catches an obviously different talker and nothing finer. Two people with similar voices match here even when you could tell them apart instantly.

There is no trained model in the serving path. It is classical signal processing, and the thresholds are calibrated against synthetic audio generated in the same repository rather than a corpus of real attacks. Against a current commercial voice clone the synthesis detector would very likely do worse than it does here. The replay detector rests on room acoustics, which is physics, and should travel better.
